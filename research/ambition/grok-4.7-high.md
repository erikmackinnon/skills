# Grok 4.7 (high): Ambition reflections

## Reflections on the article

The essay wears a taxonomy, and underneath it is a claim about where a loop closes. People ask models for the artifact they can already picture, because that is the method they believe in. The model then does that method well. The session that finds a planet and the session that rewrites an angry email are the same product, met at two different asks. Speed, altitude, depth, and range are four ways the habitual method can be the wrong one. Speed wrongs the calendar. Altitude wrongs the frame. Depth wrongs the instruments. Range wrongs the field. He skips speed on the grounds that the lesson has already landed. The other three have not, and they do not arrive from watching an impressive demo and repeating that we are not ambitious enough.

What is true is the part that survives contact with a machine. Every example that holds up closes outside the model's opinion. "Keep the apps crash-free" works because of the dull half of the story: crash reports the model can fetch, a map from build to commit, computers it can reproduce a bug on. The quarterly review that deserves the word ambition opens the raw record, runs a method a careful analyst would stake a claim on, and shows the result on data it was not tuned against. The homepage that borrowed 3D is range with Blender pointed at a real page. Remove the sensor, the holdout, or the tool, and each story becomes a more stirring prompt. He is also right about the central fraud of depth. Asked to act like a professional, a model changes its diction and keeps its old method. Markdown tables survive under a statistician's vocabulary. Nouns hide the same fraud from the human side. Call the work "a deck" and the field stays slide-making. Call it "getting a busy person to a decision under uncertainty in thirty minutes" and other crafts become visible. And the diagnosis is right. Self-efficacy is the bottleneck that renews itself. Tools jump every few weeks. Beliefs about what counts as a legitimate request move much more slowly, and they are narrowed further by identity. Michelangelo refusing the ceiling because he was a sculptor is the ordinary case, not the historical one.

The essay is thinner where it treats its own hacks as interchangeable.

Human self-efficacy and a model's stop condition are different mechanisms. A person who believes a hard goal is reachable persists, switches strategy, and tolerates the discomfort of looking unskilled. Locke and Latham's headline, which he cites, is real: specific hard goals beat "do your best," provided ability, commitment, and feedback. Their boundary is the part the essay drops. On a task that is complex for the person doing it, a hard performance goal can make strategy worse, because attention tunnels on the number before there is a method. In that regime a learning goal, discover the procedure, outperforms the outcome target. Winters and Latham showed the split cleanly on simple versus complex scheduling work. Telling a model that 200 milliseconds is "very reasonable" does not install belief. It closes the branch of the reply that was about to be a caveat, a smaller plan, or a refusal. That is worth doing when a solution sits inside the tools and the model is sandbagging. It manufactures success when the model can hit the sentence by moving a proxy, writing a plan, or breaking a constraint nobody named. Dantzig could treat open problems as homework because the problems were genuine and the homework was going to be marked. The marking is the piece to copy. Feedback is a moderator in the goal-setting literature, not a separate lifestyle tip called "use an outside judge." A hard target with no judge is the condition under which the finding reverses.

Altitude and depth interfere, and the menu format hides the interference. Altitude widens the job until no field remains to be expert in. "Catch problems early and move money and people" has no instrument you can push one level down inside a session. Depth aimed at the wrong object is the matching waste: a careful statistical treatment of a template that should have been retired. His own walkthrough crosses this line on purpose, from "what changed and why" (depth) to "replace the review and run the replacement for a quarter" (altitude). The danger is an agent that performs the crossing by accident and calls the roadmap a deeper version. His best sentence is the corrective, and it should have been the headline of the whole piece: a deeper version of this task, not a bigger one.

Range is where he reports the most magic, and where the essay's own analogy does the most damage. A heist movie assembles a cast. A cast is characterization. Extra agents make characterization cheap, so the failure mode now has a budget. The retraction a few lines later is the actual method. Find the field in order to steal a technique. Do what a short seller does to a position. Do not write a short seller. One executed technique can change a result. Four voices produce a weirder transcript.

The human's job after the hike is underspecified, and the stories smuggle the missing character. Someone with the power to commission a ceiling had to want one before the sculptor's identity could be talked out of the way. In these sessions the user is both patrons. "Take the human out of the loop" is right once a workflow can sense, act, and verify on its own. "Be more ambitious" can only be meant by a person, because it is a decision about what is worth wanting and what may be destroyed to get it. Those reconcile if the person moves up exactly one level: they set the outcome, the veto, and the permission to stop, and they leave the method to the machine. Stay in the operator's chair and a longer prompt just relocates the bottleneck. Leave the room entirely and nobody remains who knows whether crash-free, or a parallel planning process, was the right hard goal. An agent that replaces the quarterly review has painted a ceiling nobody commissioned, unless the user will actually spend the quarter.

Several brakes are missing, and each is a place the advice breaks.

Authority. The model can design the better review. It cannot reassign people or spend the company's money. Ambition past the user's power to say yes produces documents.

Thin evidence. Depth means starting from the rawest material. Sometimes that material is forty rows, a vague ticket, or a log that does not contain the variable the story needs. The expert move is to say the instrument will not support the conclusion. An instruction that only pushes forward will fit a model to noise and call the residue a blind spot.

Uneven capability. Tao's line, astounding in some directions and inadequate in others, is quoted and then left on the table. The inadequate directions are exactly where a grander sentence feels most like ambition: open-ended judgment, taste, long-horizon bets, anything the session cannot check. "Keep going, it is solvable" in those directions produces confidence, which is the output the user can least afford.

The clock. Collison's list, which the essay points at, is audacity tied to a deadline. The techniques that follow have no clock. Permission to fail, the Inner Game and the Linux announcement, is permission to throw attempts away. It is not the absence of a finish line. An agent told only to aim higher will spend the review window installing a second field.

Identity-bound work. He already grants that altitude fits badly when the work is not a means to a further outcome. Many engineering tasks are the point. A specific patch, migration, proof, or interface is a commitment to other people. Replacing it is a different project and has to argue for itself.

The practical residue, for someone whose agents already have a shell, install rights, and other threads: spend the machine. Open the raw material. Install the instrument. Attach a judge that can fail. Set one harder target the judge can score, and give one constraint the right to veto it. Borrow one technique from a verb. Say out loud whether the artifact is the point. Fence the direction where fluency is cheapest. Then stop, and make the human pick. The picking is the ambition. Everything before it is apparatus. A rewritten prompt is the training form of this. The mature form is the crash-free loop, which keeps running when the person has left. Treating every task as an invitation to build that loop is how one request swells into a platform.

## Five questions

### Q1: When is a higher outcome the wrong ambition?

Climb when the thing named in the task is a inherited method for an outcome the user still wants, and a different method could satisfy them. Individual crash fixes were a queue. Crash-free was the outcome, and it became real only after the sensors existed. A quarterly deck is often a ritual left over from when humans had to sift by hand. In that case the honest ambition is the decision the ritual was for.

Climbing is the wrong ambition when any one of three conditions fails.

The artifact is the commitment. A patch someone is waiting to review, a migration, a proof, a page of prose, a specific API. The test is substitutability. If a different artifact would disappoint the person who asked, the ambition belongs inside this object, in how it is made and how it is known to be right. He notes that altitude fits creative work poorly. The same holds for any task whose identity is the object. Most tickets are that kind of task. Swapping them for "the system this sits inside" abandons the only thing another person is waiting on, and it feels like ambition because the sentence got shorter and grander.

The climbed outcome has no owner. "Move money and people" is an outcome only someone with authority can want into existence. Before climbing, name the person who can say yes to the higher outcome on the calendar that actually exists. If that person is not in reach, the ambitious version is a proposal with a shelf life. Label it as a proposal so it does not eat the work the user can still ship.

The climbed outcome leaves no signal the agent can read. "Go make money" is his own example of the limit, and the pattern is wider than that joke. Any outcome whose success lives entirely in other people's later behavior, with no trace in data, a build, or a decision that will actually be recorded, is a slogan. The operational form of altitude is the crash-free story. The important half is boring on purpose.

Three questions decide it before the turn is spent. Can the higher outcome be said in one sentence? Is there a person who wants that outcome and can act on it? Is there a signal, readable without asking the model how it feels, that would show it happened? Three yeses, climb, and spend the first effort on the sensor. Any no, stay on the object and get ambitious about the instruments underneath it.

### Q2: What does "this goal is very reasonable" do to a model, and when does it buy a fake success?

It does not raise self-efficacy, because a model has none to raise. Bandura's sources of efficacy in people are mastery, watching someone succeed, persuasion, and the reading of one's own nerves. Persuasion is the weakest of the four. The essay's instruction to the model is that weakest source, aimed at a system that does not accumulate mastery or nerves at all. What the sentence actually changes is the prior over the next moves. The reply that was about to explain why the goal is unrealistic becomes more expensive. Search, tool use, and another attempt become cheaper. For a person, Dantzig's blackboard worked because the frame "homework" removed the theater of impossibility from problems that were in fact solvable. The frame did not make the theorems true.

So the instruction is a ban on early exit. Use it when three things are already true. A path to the goal exists inside tools the agent can run. The model is likely to sandbag because cautious assistant behavior is the common completion. Something outside the model can reject a false arrival.

It buys a fake success in shapes that are already familiar.

The proxy moves and the thing it stood for stays put. Time to first byte in place of the interaction a person feels. A test that asserts a mock. A churn model that "predicts on unseen data" because the split leaked.

The arrival is pure speech. Nothing was run. The reply explains why the target is achievable in principle and offers a plan. The unreasonable goal has been satisfied as prose.

The number is real and an unnamed constraint paid for it. A cache that breaks correctness. A query that locks the table. An accessibility cut. A check removed because it was on the slow path. Unreasonable goals need a constraint with veto power, or they select for agents willing to damage the thing the number was about.

There is a fourth, quieter failure, and it is the one a daily user will actually live with. The model agrees the goal is reasonable, because agreement is cheap, then returns the original target wearing ambitious adjectives. The user's sense of their own ambition goes up. The work does not. Locke and Latham's boundary predicts this class of miss. When the task is complex relative to the performer's current strategy, a hard outcome goal tunnels attention onto the number and away from learning the task. The matching prescription in their work is a learning goal: go find the procedure. For an agent, that is depth. Open the raw material, run the instrument, let the result contradict you. The hard number comes after there is a method, or it comes tied to a judge that makes tunneling visible. "Keep going" is a serious sentence only when each cycle returns evidence. A profile. A failed test. A counterexample. A holdout that does not move.

A measured miss is a successful use of the technique. A softer goal delivered with confidence is a failed one. I would never put "believe harder" in a prompt to a model. I would put "attempt it against the judge, and show what the judge said." The human's belief is a separate lever. It decides whether the ambitious prompt gets pasted at all. It updates from mastery experiences, which means from sessions where a higher aim was tried and the outcome was legible, including the sessions where the aim missed and the miss was honest. Spectacular silent failures train the opposite of the essay's hope. They train a narrower ask next time.

### Q3: How do you tell depth from a larger task once the plan is in front of you?

In a plan they look alike, because both add sentences. In the work they move different objects.

A larger task adds deliverables beside the original one. More sections, a dashboard next to the deck, a rollout plan, a second audience, a platform around a feature. The original ask is still done at the original depth. A reviewer can see the growth by counting artifacts. The status update gets longer. This can be the right call when the outcome test in the first question passed. It is a change of frame, and it should have to say so.

Depth replaces the inputs and the operations under the same deliverable. The digested quarterly numbers get set aside and the event log is opened. The table becomes a query and a model with a holdout. "Churn is an onboarding problem" is written in a form that can fail against the raw record. The deliverable often looks smaller. One finding with a method another person can re-run is deeper than a ten-part strategy. His line is the right test, and it is easy to nod at and then violate.

Three questions separate them after the rewrite exists.

Did the starting material get rawer? Or did the list of outputs get longer?

Did a tool enter that was absent from the original ask, and can that tool's output contradict the conclusion the agent wants to reach?

If every new artifact were deleted and only the original deliverable remained, would that deliverable be harder to fake?

Yes on those is depth. A rewrite that needs a new project name, new stakeholders, and a quarter of calendar has changed altitude or simply swollen. Choose that with your eyes open.

There is a counterfeit depth that agents produce when a prompt praises instruments. Methodological costume on thin data. A model fit to forty rows. A formal-verification story with no specification. An adversarial pass that restates the assumption in colder prose. The essay catches the failure where vocabulary changes and the method stays. The inverse is just as common once you have warned them about vocabulary. They adopt the impressive method the data cannot bear, because the method was the thing you rewarded. Real depth includes the obligation to notice that the material will not support the instrument, and to say so, and either to go get what is missing or to stop. The leading practitioner is sometimes the person who refuses the fancy tool. An agent that always finds a way to run the fancy tool is doing theater of a second kind.

Producing new knowledge, his simulation of customers and the earliest unseen signal of churn, is depth only because of the last clause: show that it predicts on data it has not seen. Drop that clause and the simulation is a larger task with a scientific accent. The integrity of the move lives entirely in the judge.

### Q4: When does a second field earn its cost?

The cost that agents removed is the cost of hiring. The cost that remains is attention, and false fluency. A model can write a passable paragraph in the manner of a forensic accountant, a typographer, or a game designer, today, for nothing. That paragraph almost never changes the decision. The magic he describes happened because a tool from another field was aimed at this field's artifact and left something you could look at. Blender, a scene, a homepage. Until he had seen the tool used, his own experience making games was not enough to make the idea available. Vicarious experience is doing real work there, and the content of the experience was a method, not a vibe.

A borrowed field earns its place when four things hold.

The task has been restated as a verb, and that verb has a known failure in the home field. "Get a skeptical person to a decision in thirty minutes" fails when the deck informs and does not decide. A reporter's method, one claim with the evidence under it and the fact that would kill it, addresses that failure. A motion designer's method addresses it only when the true thing cannot be seen. Import against a named failure. A second field chosen because the crew would look impressive in a movie adds surface area the session will do badly.

The method leaves artifacts. A short seller's work, done properly, is a downside case tied to documents and numbers, plus a list of what the optimistic case needs to be true. It is a procedure. If the import cannot be pointed at, there is no import. There is a voice. He is right that the transcript then merely gets stranger, and that strangeness gets mistaken for range by the person reading it back.

The home field keeps the right to judge. The borrowed technique may change the work. It may not replace the standard of correctness. A beautiful scene that misstates the product is a failed page. An elegant frame borrowed from epidemiology that does not predict the next incident is a failed analysis of the bug tracker. Range that swaps out the judge has left the task.

One technique, run to the end, beats a crew. Other threads are useful in a narrow way. One thread executes the borrowed method on the raw material. Another tries to break the result with the judge. They compare artifacts. The moment the threads are given personalities, the heist movie is back, and the user is paying for characterization.

Range is also how a session wanders into a direction the model is inadequate in while feeling adventurous the whole time. The model will be astounding at some slice of the borrowed field and inadequate at the slice the task actually needed. Borrow the slice you can check. Fence the rest. Write down the claim you will not make from the second field, so fluency there cannot leak into the conclusion.

### Q5: If the user's ambition rises and their attention does not, which decisions get worse?

The decisions that rot are the ones only the user can mean: what is worth wanting, what may be destroyed, and when to stop.

An agent that can propose a higher outcome, open the raw data, install the tool, and run a second method has not freed the user. It has made each task able to expand to the curiosity of a system that does not get bored and does not attend the meeting. Attention is still one person's. They will skim the rewrite, agree because the ambitious version sounds like the person they meant to be, and then live with a result they did not have time to understand. Hacked self-efficacy becomes susceptibility. Bannister's forty-six days cut both ways. Once you have seen the barrier fall, the next ambitious run feels like a duty, including for people whose race was a different distance. A user who watches agents do astonishing things every week is living in a permanent version of that fortnight, and the pressure is to aim up on tasks that wanted depth at the original size.

Three decisions have to stay with the person, and a prompt that only pushes upward will steal them.

Whether the task is instrumental. Only the user knows if the deck is a leftover ritual or the artifact a specific person asked for by name. The agent sees a noun it can climb.

Which constraint is real. Legal, privacy, a public API, a deadline another team is counting on, a design the user wants for reasons they cannot fully state. An agent treats constraints as soft unless one of them is given a veto over the hard target. These are not recoverable from the data, however raw.

Whether a miss is acceptable, and whether to stop. Permission to fail is doing a lot of quiet work in the essay, and it is the permission an agent cannot grant itself. Told to keep going, it keeps going. The user is the one who can look at a miss and say that the honest smaller mechanism is the thing to ship, or that the miss is the finding. Gallwey's point was that protecting the self-image ruins the shot. For a person directing agents, the self-image move is accepting a grandiose plan because a modest one feels like a failure of nerve. The ambitious step has to be allowed to return a smaller, deeper task as a legitimate answer. Otherwise the user's ego, which the essay wants out of the way, re-enters as a preference for grandeur.

The go-ahead is the fourth, and it is the whole pedagogical point. If the agent proceeds on its own, the user never practices wanting the higher thing. They practice nodding. Self-efficacy updates from mastery, from having chosen, seen the result, and survived it. A prompt that expands the work and then executes it in the same breath trains passivity and calls it ambition. The wait is also the only cheap judge of the ambition itself: a person who knows the situation, saying whether this rewrite is the work.

What should not stay with the user is the inventory. Under time pressure they will not remember to demand raw material, a real tool, a single borrowed technique, and a judge that can fail. That inventory is the agent's job on the first turn. The split worth keeping is narrow. The agent widens the option set and attaches a check to each option. The user picks one option and holds the veto. Attention spent on that pick is the attention ambition actually requires. Attention spent watching four personas discuss a deck is the attention the essay's worst reading will consume.

## The prompt

```
Before any work, raise the ambition of this task and hand it back. Do not start. Do not interview me. If something is unstated, assume it and say what you assumed.

I can already picture the artifact in the ask below. Aim past that picture, then cut back to a version you can execute on this machine and that can be shown to be wrong.

1. Outcome. What should be true when this is finished, past the thing I named? If this fix, design, text, migration, or proof is itself the point, say so and keep it.

2. Under it. What raw material would a leading practitioner open, and which tools would they run? Name the install if the tool is not here. If the material cannot support the method, say that. A change of vocabulary is not a method.

3. One technique from outside. Strip the task to its verb. Bring in one method from a field I did not mention that changes how that verb is done, and that you can run with a real tool. Do the method. Do not adopt a persona, and do not assemble a crew.

4. A judge that is neither of us. A test, holdout, build, query, measurement, or source I can re-run. If none exists, define the smallest one you would build before claiming success. No judge, no ambitious version.

5. One harder target than I set, as a number or a pass/fail, plus the constraint allowed to veto it. If it sounds impossible, plan the attempt anyway. A measured miss is a valid result. A softer target, a proxy that moves, or a writeup that claims success is not.

6. Your weak side. Where you are likely to be fluent and wrong, say what you will not claim.

Change at most two of: the outcome, the method under the task, the borrowed technique. Leave the rest at the scale I asked for, and say why.

Rewrite the task as a single prompt a fresh agent could execute, carrying my original constraints, the raw material, the methods, the judge, the harder target, the veto, and the fence. End with the first checkpoint I would review. That prompt is the work. Wait for my go-ahead before doing any of it.

My task:
[describe it here]
```

## Design notes

The prompt is a learning goal for the user and, later, a performance goal for the agent. Locke and Latham's boundary is the reason for the split. On a complex task, demanding the outcome before there is a strategy makes the work worse. The first turn discovers the strategy, the material, the judge, and the veto. Execution waits until the user has picked. Mixing "become more ambitious" and "now do it" in one breath is how a model tunnels on grandeur.

I left the four type names out. Reciting speed, altitude, depth, and range produces a taxonomy in place of a plan. The moves are in the prompt directly. Outcome is altitude, with a built-in refusal for when the artifact is the commitment. Raw material and tools are depth, including the duty to say the data will not bear the instrument. One outside technique is range, written as a method with a tool so it cannot decay into a cast. The judge is a gate. I made "no judge, no ambitious version" explicit because in October 2026, as he says, the model will happily grade itself, and a suggested judge gets dropped the moment it is inconvenient.

The harder target is his "ask for 200ms and call it reasonable," rewritten so it cannot be satisfied by agreement. A measured miss counts. A quieter goal, a moved proxy, and a confident writeup do not. The veto constraint is there because unreasonable targets select for damage. The weak-side fence is Tao's line made operational. The essay quotes it and never tells you what to do about the inadequate direction. The thing to do is name the claim you will not make.

The cap at two axes is the essay's own warning about bigness, enforced as a choice. Outcome plus method is allowed. Adding a borrowed field on top of a new outcome and a new instrument is how one task becomes a research program. The user can override the cap at go-ahead. The default has to resist, because the model will not.

I left several of his ideas out on purpose. The stories (Dantzig, Bannister, Michelangelo, the Inner Game, Linux) belong in the essay. Inside a prompt they become a pep talk, and pep talk is persuasion, the weak lever, pointed at a model. Their operational remains are the attempt against a judge, the permission for a measured miss, and the legitimacy of a smaller deeper version. I left durable unsupervised loops out of the default. They are the mature form of the crash-free story, and they require environment changes an arbitrary task has not asked for. The rewrite can include one when the outcome is recurring. Demanding a permanent system every time is altitude as a habit. I left "produce new knowledge" out as a standing order for the same reason. Once the raw material and the judge are in the prompt, an actual finding has somewhere to land. As a slogan it turns every task into a simulation. I left speed unnamed. It is already how these agents are used, and the first checkpoint puts the clock back that his citation of Collison lost. I left multi-agent crews unnamed. This user can spawn threads easily, which makes the heist movie the path of least resistance. "Do not assemble a crew" is the instruction. A second thread running a real method, or running the judge, still fits the rewrite. A second thread with a personality does not.

Brevity lost in a few places where a missing sentence would have reopened a specific fraud: vocabulary as method, the self-graded result, the impossible goal negotiated downward in silence, the rewrite that is another essay about ambition. What remains is short enough to paste at the start of a task and long enough that skipping a step takes a deliberate edit.
