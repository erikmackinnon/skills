# Writing and communication

Read this when the task is mostly prose: essays, documentation, emails, announcements, posts, scripts, talks, cover letters.

## Outcome

- **Ask what the reader should do, believe or feel afterwards.** That is the outcome. The text is only the means.
- **Sometimes the writing is the point.** An apology, a personal essay, or anything in the author's own voice can't be replaced with something else. Then ambition goes into the craft, not into changing the deliverable.
- **For documentation,** the outcome is that readers find answers and succeed at their task. Sometimes the ambitious move is a fix that makes a page unnecessary, such as a clearer error message, a better default or an example that runs.

## Raw material

Start from:
- the readers' actual questions, support tickets and search queries
- replies to earlier pieces, and transcripts
- the source documents the piece draws on
- the author's previous writing, for their voice

Don't start from a template or the previous draft alone.

## Methods and tools

| Instead of… | A strong writer or editor would… |
|---|---|
| Starting to draft | Write the one-sentence point first, then the outline that serves it |
| Polishing sentences | Reverse-outline the draft: one line per paragraph, then check the order holds up |
| Adding words | Cut a third and see what was lost |
| Guessing what readers need | Gather the real questions readers ask and check the piece answers each one |
| Proofreading by eye | Read it aloud, or run a readability and fact check against the sources |
| One draft | Two genuinely different openings or structures, compared against criteria written in advance |

## Other fields worth borrowing from

- **Journalism:** lead with the news, cite every claim, and decide what to leave out.
- **Screenwriting:** stakes, structure and cutting into a scene late.
- **UX writing:** test whether the reader can complete the task.
- **Teaching:** worked examples before abstractions, and check that the reader understood.
- **Rhetoric:** the strongest objection, answered before the reader raises it.

## Judges

Writing rarely has a hard judge, so build the best partial ones you can and be honest about the rest:
- **A reader test.** A real member of the intended audience, or a set of representative questions the piece must answer.
- **A cold restatement.** Someone, or a separate agent given only the text, restates the main point. Does it match the intended one?
- **A blind comparison.** Compare against the modest version using criteria written before either draft.
- **A fact check** of every claim against its source.

Label what's verified, what's inferred and what's a matter of taste. When you can't judge quality, show small samples early so the user can calibrate, instead of delivering one large polished piece they can only accept or reject whole.

## Targets and vetoes

- **Targets:** half the length with the same content; a cold reader gets the point within 30 seconds; every reader question answered.
- **Vetoes:** the author's voice, factual accuracy, tone and relationship, any length or format that's required.

## Traps

- **Longer, more sections, more ornament.**
- **The model rating its own prose as "compelling".**
- **Replacing the author's voice** with generic fluency.
- **Turning a personal piece into a "content strategy".**
